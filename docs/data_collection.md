# Data collection: hardware, protocol and metadata

This page separates **what was reported**, **what was verified from the CSV files**, and **what is unresolved**. The machine-readable copy is the `hardware`, `protocol` and `participant_metadata` blocks of `configs/default.yaml` (each with its source). The CSV-level checks are in `reports/audit/hardware_*.csv` (generated by `python -m src.pipeline`).

Source of all "reported" items: the person responsible for the hardware (the *hardware collaborator*). Not independently verified. **Not available:** raw int16 register values, firmware source, register read-back/initialisation logs, board photographs, the recording order of the legs, per-trial metadata.

## 1. Confirmed hardware configuration (collaborator-reported)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Microcontroller | ESP32 |
| IMU module / chip | GY-521 / MPU6050 (3-axis accelerometer + 3-axis gyroscope) |
| Interface | I2C; SDA = GPIO 21, SCL = GPIO 22 |
| Accelerometer range | +-2 g |
| Gyroscope range | +-250 deg/s |
| Sampling interval / frequency | 0.02 s / 50 Hz |
| Duration / samples per leg | about 10 s / about 500 |
| Conversion (documented firmware formulas) | accelerometer: value (g) = raw / 16384; gyroscope: value (deg/s) = raw / 131 |

These factors are the MPU6050 sensitivities for the stated full-scale settings. The description alone does not show that the sensor was initialised to those ranges in every recording, or that the firmware exported exactly what was documented.

## 2. Collaborator-reported mounting and protocol

* Module on the shin, about one breadboard-length below the knee.
* Module **inverted, its top facing the ground**. The anatomical meaning of sensor X, Y and Z is **not confirmed** (no board markings or photographs available). No axis is assumed to be vertical, forward or lateral.
* **The same physical sensor was used for both legs.** Consequence: device gain/offset differences cancel in a left-right comparison. Not removed: re-mounting differences between legs, orientation, timing/phase, measurement range.
* Barefoot, flat surface, about 10 s per leg, a 3 s warning before walking straight, first step with the leg opposite to the sensor leg. Described as consistent across participants (not verifiable from the CSVs).
* Left and right recordings are separate trials; they are **not** assumed to be time-synchronised or gait-phase aligned.

## 4. Verified directly from the CSV files (consistency checks only)

* One schema in all 24 files; six sensor channels plus `Time_ms`.
* Rows 498-500 (all within 5 of 500); estimated sampling frequency 50.0 Hz in all recordings; last timestamp exactly 10 000 ms. A handful of dropped samples (40 ms steps) and one late start (Sub06 right, first sample 40 ms).
* Gyroscope values: 100 % lie on the grid `round(k/131, 3)` for an integer k (about 13 % would by chance), none outside the int16 range. This agrees with the division by 131 and indicates no later averaging or non-integer offset subtraction. Accelerometer values cannot be tested this way (the CSV has 4 decimals, a step of 0.0001 that is coarser than 1/16384 = 0.000061, so any 4-decimal value is attainable), so the accelerometer scale is checked through gravity instead: |a| in the quietest 0.5 s of each recording averages about 1.02 g.
* The observed extremes (acc -2.0000 / +1.9999 g; gyro -250.137 / +250.130 deg/s) are exactly the values for raw counts -32768 and +32767 under the documented formulas.
* Accelerometer Y is the gravity-dominant axis in all 24 recordings with a negative sign (mean unit component about -0.95).

## 5. Assumptions and unresolved items

1. Whether the values at the extremes are **hardware saturation** (the physical rate exceeded the configured range), a firmware clamp, or something else. The data are consistent with output reaching the register limit (plateaus of several samples at exactly the limit, smooth ramps into them, no wrap-around of the dominant events) but this cannot be confirmed without raw counts, firmware and a bench test.
2. The mapping of X, Y, Z to anatomical axes, and whether the mount orientation was identical on both legs (static gravity directions of a participant's two recordings differ by a median of about 11 deg, up to 39 deg; this mixes posture and mounting).
3. Zero-rate offsets: no recording contains a clean rest period (11/24 already show movement within 100 ms), so gyroscope bias cannot be estimated.
4. Order of legs, walking speed, and whether the 3 s warning preceded the recording start.
5. Whether `Time_ms` comes from a hardware clock or a nominal schedule (all values are exact multiples of 20 ms).
